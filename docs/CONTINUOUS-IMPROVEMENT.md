# Improve usefulness, clarity and reliability from evidence

Contract version 1, adopted October 26, 2026. Changes to this contract need
independent review and a version increment. Operator authorization and AGENTS.md
binding safeguards take precedence; this contract cannot override them.

The aim is useful, independently checked work that saves maintainer time.
More tests, longer reports and more proposals are not success by themselves.
This loop improves the process; it cannot guarantee a discovery or a measurable
improvement every week. An honest unchanged or inconclusive result is valid.

## Before selecting work

Read the current scope, [queue](../config/improvements.json), latest report,
latest learning record and [lessons](../LESSONS.md). When authorized, review
correspondence privately. For each substantive request or promised artifact,
record its disposition: done with evidence, active with next checkpoint,
deliberately deferred with reason, or a necessary question. Reconcile earlier
candidate adoption at exact source/proof pins. Silence is not acceptance;
absence of adoption is not evidence that a proposal was useless.

Keep private message IDs, quotations, preferences and delivery records outside
this repository. Public records describe neutral technical requirements and
their evidence. Operators without mail access use the public scope and state
that external feedback was unavailable; they must not infer it.

Choose work by explicit requests, consequence of a possible error, remaining
uncertainty, likely maintainer usefulness, effort and available prerequisites.
Explain the choice briefly. Prefer closing a promised useful artifact over
starting another speculative track. Preserve justified deferred work. Keep
three genuine ranked priorities when available, never invent findings to fill
slots. Reuse unchanged evidence with its original date and configuration.
For otherwise comparable tasks, prefer an explicit current request, then the
higher-consequence unresolved risk; record a reason when choosing differently.

## Define success, then execute

Before work, name the next artifact or obligation, the check that would accept
it, its scope, budget and stop condition. Success might be a checked patch,
useful corpus addition, closed proof premise, or a precise negative result
that prevents an expensive unproductive search. Report failed attempts and
unresolved dependencies; do not relabel activity as completion.

Evaluate the last process change when its stated checkpoint arrives: supported
benefit, inconclusive, harmful, or not yet exercised. Cite the observation.
Do not rerun an unchanged scientific campaign just to measure a process change.
At most one small process improvement may be added to the week's existing
budget, and only when justified by an observed problem or explicit request.
No improvement is required if the evidence does not justify one.
Process work ranks below useful technical work and is dropped first when the
budget is tight. Before trying it, state the predicted benefit, where to check
it and what would justify reverting or retiring it. After two inconclusive
evaluations, explicitly narrow, defer or retire the experiment, or record why
another observation is justified. Never automatically roll back a scientific
safeguard merely because its benefit was not observed.

## Review for correctness and usefulness

Independent review must inspect both the scientific claims and their value to
the intended reader. Check source/model binding, mathematical premises and
units, reproducible receipts, negative controls where relevant, prior findings,
unanswered requests, candidate adoption and the limitations of the tested scope.
Record what the reviewer actually saw or executed. A model verdict is advisory;
independently close every required condition before claiming clearance.
Scientific correctness and gate compliance are blocking checks; usefulness and
clarity review follows them and must not dilute or replace them.

Check the communication separately: can a reader understand the result and
its consequence from the lead? Is the next action explicit, or is no action
needed? Does each claimed improvement have evidence? Could an unfamiliar team
member reproduce or apply the artifact from the linked material? Are tests,
proofs, conditional results, untested configurations and unknowns distinct?

Email remains a short delivery summary, normally 80–150 words, with details in
the report. Necessary witness/context may justify more; record why privately.
Lead with scoped health and what materially changed. Put confirmed failures
and useful fixes first. Do not fill the email with process metrics, repeated
backlogs, acknowledgements or unchanged requests. Apply the private operator's
send/no-send policy; this document grants no correspondence permission.
Mention process changes in email only if they materially change the result
the recipient receives or an action requested of them.

## Record learning and check it next time

Add one short `LEARNING.md` beside each new weekly output using
[the template](../templates/LEARNING.md). Ad hoc work may append its learning to
the next scheduled review instead of creating another public process report.
The latest learning record is the newest `weekly/*/LEARNING.md`, or
`docs/learning/2026-10-26/LEARNING.md` before the first scheduled review.
Keep request/response mapping and personal feedback private; the public excerpt
contains technical outcomes, public adoption evidence and reusable lessons only.
The [initial record](learning/2026-10-26/LEARNING.md) starts the loop; do not
backfill or reinterpret historical reports as if this process ran then.
Link evidence rather than duplicating it. Record the intended outcome, actual
outcome, prior-change evaluation, and either one justified improvement or
"no justified change." Each proposed change needs an owner, acceptance check,
next checkpoint and revert/retire condition. Keep unverified benefits unverified.

Use a few descriptive observations when available: a delivered artifact became
usable or was adopted; a repeated error was prevented; a later correction was
needed; reproduction became clearer; an unnecessary run or repeated request
was avoided. Counts need a defined scope and denominator. Missing observations
are unknown, not zero. There is no composite quality score or required upward
trend. Adoption and favorable replies are useful signals, not correctness proofs.

Promote only durable, supported lessons into `LESSONS.md`; retain evidence and
note supersession rather than rewriting history. Every fourth completed weekly
review, within the same budget, check whether rules are redundant or stale and
whether stalled tasks should be narrowed, deferred or retired with reasons.
If this maintenance cannot fit, record a checkpoint instead of expanding work.
Count completed scheduled reviews since adoption, excluding ad hoc, skipped or
aborted runs; retain the ordinal in the learning record. Include LESSONS.md in
pruning, merging duplicates with a supersession note. A finalized reviewed report
with explicit incomplete scientific checks may count as a completed review;
this does not turn those checks into passes.

## Boundaries that learning cannot change

Preserve pauses, supported configurations, validation strength, protected
sources, source/proof/measurement/delivery watermarks, privacy, independent
review, one-runner coordination, CPU accounting and clean handoffs. The existing
budget still covers one primary task and at most one bounded bivariate task.
Process edits cannot grant permissions, increase cadence/budget, add recipients,
resume paused scope or create another scheduler. Review changed instructions
before adopting them; test changed executable behavior with appropriate positive
and negative controls. Never change historical evidence or lower a validation
standard to make the new process appear successful.
