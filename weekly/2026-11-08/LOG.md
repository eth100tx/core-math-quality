# Near-one binary64 log: what the stopping test needs

At the [pinned implementation](https://gitlab.inria.fr/core-math/core-math/-/blob/660067dfd3e751c8f07acc31c899ff410aa7206e/src/binary64/log/log.c#L653), the dispatch bit limits select `1−2^-9 ≤ x < 1+2^-9`. The exact-one branch returns +0. Otherwise Sterbenz's lemma makes `r=x−1` exact; positive r is at least 2^-52 and negative |r| at least 2^-53. Both are multiples of 2^-53. The two input intervals and the lattice constraint are parenthesized explicitly in our Gappa goals. The value claim below concerns this fast return only.

**1. Real approximation.** Let P be the exact degree-seven polynomial whose six hexadecimal coefficients follow the leading term r in C. [The checker](check_log.py) verifies both input-file hashes and the six literals in C against Gappa. Divide the difference between P and the degree-12 Taylor polynomial for log(1+r) by r, extending it at zero. For ρ=1/512, the omitted series after division is at most ρ^12/[13(1−ρ)]. On 128 equal cells the checker translates the difference polynomial to the center of each cell and sums the absolute coefficient bounds. A separate calculation bounds it by the largest absolute Bernstein coefficient on each of 32 cells. Both use exact rationals and include the tail; each proves

`|P(r)−log(1+r)| < 2^-70 |r|.`

This is an allowance in units of |r|, not the source's tighter decimal estimate relative to |log(1+r)|. Neither a sampled norm nor Sollya's exploratory norm is needed. An intentionally changed coefficient fails the target.

**2. Floating-point evaluation.** The [Gappa receipt](log.json) separates the real polynomial error from the error in computing h and l. We first checked two operation graphs: (a) one rounding at each of the five polynomial multiply-adds and each inner endpoint, as in `logtiny.g`; (b) individually rounded products and sums at all those sites. Both round r² and the final product t before h is formed. No assumption that FastTwoSum is an exact transform under directed rounding is needed: for each graph and rounding mode, Gappa proves `1/2 ≤ h/r ≤ 2`, so h−r is exact by Sterbenz, and the low part is the rounded difference between t and that exact subtraction. A third graph follows GCC 13.3 compiled for x86-64-v3 with fast contraction. Its disassembly actually fuses t into **both** the high and residual operations: the mathematical intermediate t is the *exact* product of the rounded r² and the rounded inner polynomial value p2 (p2a in Gappa), rather than a rounded product. The Gappa variant changes that definition and verifies the same Sterbenz condition. In all three graphs every mode establishes

`|(h+l)−P(r)| ≤ 29·2^-65 |r|.`

For the rounding of the two inner endpoints relative to exact l±eps·r, the 2^-103 |r| target succeeds for nearest, up and down in every graph, and also for toward-zero in the GCC fused-residual graph. Our direct goals at that strength did **not** close for toward-zero in the other two graphs. In those graphs the weaker 2^-80 |r| goal succeeds there. A failed tactic is not a counterexample to the tighter bound.

**3. Why the weaker bound suffices.** In units of |r|, eps is exactly 465·2^-69 and the floating evaluation allowance is 464·2^-69. Even using 2^-80 for *all* endpoint errors leaves
`eps − 29·2^-65 − 2^-70 − 2^-80 = 2^-70 − 2^-80 > 0`.
Thus before the final rounding of h plus each endpoint the two real sums straddle log(1+r); their order reverses for negative r. Monotonicity of the selected rounding mode certifies the common result whenever the two rounded sums are equal. If they differ, the code takes its accurate fallback, outside this proof.

**Boundary.** This is a mathematical certificate for these three operation graphs under IEEE binary64 evaluation in a single mode. It does not encompass arbitrary mixtures of contraction, unexamined ways of rounding or reusing t, excess precision, flags/errno or the accurate fallback. The [baseline listings](log-assembly.json) show separate arithmetic in the inspected GCC 13.3 and Clang 18.1.3 builds. The [v3 fast-contraction listings](log-assembly-v3.json) show different near-one arithmetic from the same C: GCC fuses the high and residual, while Clang still uses separate operations. These are compilation and inspection only, without v3 numerical execution. The listings are supplementary evidence, not formal compiler verification. No new wrong numerical value is alleged.

The [standalone GCC toward-zero goal](gcc-v3-fused-residual-zr-sum.g) is one example suitable for inspection or pickup. To replay, obtain `log.c`, `dint.h`, and `logtiny.g` at the implementation pin. Run `python3 check_log.py /path/to/src/binary64/log` with Gappa (here 1.4.1); add `--emit goals` to retain each concrete obligation, and `python3 inspect_log.py /path/to/src/binary64/log` for the strict Linux listings; append `v3-contract-fast` for the v3 comparison. Read each goal's return code: expected nonclosures are recorded, not converted into passes.
